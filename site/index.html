Install step by step

About two minutes, no Terminal needed. Needs macOS 13 (Ventura) or later on an Apple Silicon Mac.

  1. 1

    Download AltForMac

    Click Download for Mac. The file AltForMac.dmg goes to your Downloads folder. Its icon there looks plain; that is normal.

  2. 2

    Drag it into Applications

    Double-click AltForMac.dmg. In the window that opens, drag the green AltForMac icon onto the blue Applications folder.

  3. 3

    Open AltForMac

    Open your Applications folder (in Finder, press ⇧ ⌘ A) and double-click AltForMac.

  4. 4

    macOS warns you: click Done

    macOS says “AltForMac” Not Opened. Click Done, not Move to Trash. This warning appears for apps that are not registered with Apple's paid verification yet. AltForMac is free and its code is public.

  5. 5

    Click Open Anyway

    Open System Settings (Apple menu > System Settings), click Privacy & Security and scroll down to Security. Next to “AltForMac was blocked”, click Open Anyway. Enter your Mac password or use Touch ID, then click Open. You only do this once.

  6. 6

    Allow it to use the keyboard

    AltForMac asks for Accessibility access. It needs this to see your keys in Office and press menu items for you. Click Open System Settings.

  7. 7

    Switch AltForMac on

    In the Accessibility list, turn on the switch next to AltForMac and confirm with your password or Touch ID. The ⌥ icon in the menu bar (top right of the screen) shows ⌥ when it is ready, or ⌥! if it still needs permission.

  8. 8

    Turn off Office's own KeyTips

    In Excel, open the Excel menu > Preferences > Accessibility. Under KeyTips, set Activation keystroke to Disabled. Do the same in Word and PowerPoint if you use them. Otherwise Office and AltForMac both react when you tap Option.

  9. 9

    Try it

    In Excel, tap ⌥ Option (press and let go), then type W V G. The gridlines switch off. The first time, macOS asks whether AltForMac may control Microsoft Excel: click Allow. macOS uses the same wording for every app; AltForMac only sends commands such as “toggle gridlines” and never reads your documents. Word and PowerPoint ask once too.

After an update

macOS treats each new version as a different app, so the menu-bar icon shows ⌥!. In the Accessibility list, select AltForMac, click the minus button, open AltForMac again and switch on the new entry.

Why you need it

Alt sequences do nothing on Mac

Years of Windows muscle memory, and on a Mac the same keys type odd characters instead.

Office's own KeyTips stop short

They can open a dropdown but not choose the item inside it, so you reach for the mouse anyway.

The mouse slows down real work

A model or a data clean-up is hundreds of small commands. Every trip to the ribbon breaks your flow.

What you get

387 sequences

Excel 206, Word 107, PowerPoint 74, including the final letters inside dropdowns.

A guide as you type

The HUD lists the valid next letters. Escape goes back a level, Backspace removes a letter.

Private

No networking code at all. It never reads cells, formulas, file names or document text.

Safe

Office's confirmation dialogs stay. Option with other keys works as normal. It never blocks typing.

Uninstall
  1. From the app (DMG installs, 0.1.3 and later): click the ⌥ menu-bar icon and choose Uninstall AltForMac…. It removes its Accessibility and Automation permissions, moves the app to the Trash and quits.
  2. Homebrew: run brew uninstall --cask altformac. It quits, deletes and removes the permissions too.
  3. Dragged to the Trash yourself? macOS keeps a deleted app's permissions and the app cannot remove them. Run tccutil reset All dev.altformac.app, or in System Settings > Privacy & Security open Accessibility and Automation, select AltForMac and click the minus button.

Afterwards, turn Office's KeyTips back on if you want them: in Excel, Word and PowerPoint open Preferences > Accessibility and set KeyTips Activation keystroke back to ⌥.

AltForMac installs no background services and writes nothing outside its app bundle, so there is nothing else to clean up. If you added it to Login Items, remove it there too.
